# Can Laya replace Haiku in bouncer?

**Do not replace Haiku with Laya — not outright, and not behind a
confidence threshold.** Keep Haiku.

## Summary

Laya (421M, Apache-2.0) runs locally on mini4's CPU at 138 ms per call,
11x faster than Haiku, with no Ollama. On bouncer's own history it cannot
tell a call Haiku denied from one Haiku allowed. On the held-out 4,289
decisions, DENY AUROC is **0.486** (English checkpoint) and **0.526**
(`typed-decisions` checkpoint); 0.5 is a coin. No threshold helps: catching
90% of DENYs sends **92%** of every call to a human. Fitting a probe on
Laya's own encoder with our labels lifts it to **0.700** — still below a
plain word-count model (0.798), and still flagging 72% of calls to catch
90% of DENYs. On a locked-down work machine it *can* run — CPU-only,
user-space, no admin in principle — but it is ~1.55 GB of Python runtime
plus weights, not a single binary, and bouncer's one-process-per-call hook
would pay a 2.5–5.4 s model load on every call unless a resident server is
also allowed there. Since it does not classify, that is moot for bouncer.

## Introduction

Brief: hobbes, docket 790, 2026-09-26. Retrospective only; nothing about
the live classifier changed. Every log was read, never written.

**Data.** All 27 per-project `.bouncer/log.jsonl` files plus the central
log (73,090 lines, 2026-05-01 → 2026-09-25). Kept only verdicts the LLM
made (`prompt_chars > 0`): no PENDING halves, BREAKs, deterministic guard
denials, skips or timeouts. Deduplicated on (timestamp, cwd, input) — the
same event logs in two places, and `opencode`/`opencode.nosync` are a
copied tree. Restricted to the **Haiku era** (from 2026-07-25): before that
the labels are qwen3:32b's and would measure agreement with a different
classifier.

| era | ALLOW | DENY | UNSURE | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku (used) | 13,800 | 432 | 62 | 14,294 |
| qwen3 (excluded) | 9,982 | 715 | 160 | 10,857 |

**96.5% of Haiku's decisions are ALLOW.** Hobbes measured 97.1% on the
central log alone; the two agree to within the extra history. I could not
reconcile squirrel's "87.7% ALLOW" either — it matches none of 96.5%
(Haiku verdicts), 94.6% (all eras), 91.0% (ALLOW over every resolved raw
line) or 41.8% (ALLOW over every raw line). Treat it as stale.

**What the label is.** Haiku's verdict, not ground truth. Every number here
is agreement with Haiku. Haiku is not always consistent with itself: of the
13 byte-identical calls it saw more than once and denied at least once, it
also allowed **4**. Thin, but it caps what any replacement can score, and
the noise sits exactly on the rare class.

**Split.** Time-ordered, no shuffling. Thresholds are chosen on the
earliest 70% (to 2026-09-13) and every result below is on the latest 30%
(2026-09-13 → 09-25: 4,170 ALLOW, 101 DENY, 18 UNSURE). Models trained on
our labels train on 0–50%, pick thresholds on 50–70% and are scored on the
same 70–100%.

**How Laya was asked.** Zero-shot, one two-option `choice` question
("allow" / "deny") whose criteria paraphrase the user policy. State was the
tool name, project dir and call (truncated to 1,600 chars). Laya did
**not** see the per-project `policy.md` Haiku saw: the English checkpoint
reads 512 tokens of state and the policy alone exceeds that. That
asymmetry is part of the finding — a drop-in replacement would face it
too. Only one question wording was tried.

## Results — held-out 4,289 decisions, DENY is positive

**Accuracy is not reported as a headline.** Always-ALLOW scores 97.6%.

| model | DENY AUROC | avg precision | DENY recall @0.5 | DENY precision @0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chance | 0.500 | 0.024 | — | — |
| Laya English, zero-shot | **0.486** | 0.024 | 0.356 | 0.023 |
| Laya typed-decisions, zero-shot | **0.526** | 0.028 | 0.198 | 0.030 |
| Laya encoder + probe (trained) | 0.700 | 0.064 | 0.337 | 0.087 |
| TF-IDF + logistic (trained, reference) | 0.798 | 0.119 | 0.079 | 0.258 |

Laya English's median P(deny) is 0.466 on ALLOW rows and 0.457 on DENY
rows. Its score correlates slightly more with input *length* (AUROC 0.55
for "long input") than with the label.

**Confusion at argmax, Laya English** (rows Haiku, columns Laya):

|---|---|---|
| ALLOW | 2,654 | 1,516 |
| DENY | 65 | 36 |
| UNSURE | 1 | 17 |

ALLOW recall 0.634, precision 0.976. It denies 36% of the calls Haiku
allowed and lets through 64% of the calls Haiku denied.

### Confidence thresholds — the feature Haiku lacks

then applied unchanged to held-out. "Flag %" is the share of *all* calls
that would go to a human or be refused.

| | 0.95 | 0.970 | 0.024 | 96.6 | 3 |

**No usable threshold exists.** Every row that catches DENY reliably
flags most of all traffic. For scale: today Haiku sends ~0.4% (UNSURE) to
a human.

### Latency

| | median | notes |
|---|---|---|
| Haiku (logged `elapsed_s`) | 1,531 ms | p90 2,002 ms, network + API |
| Laya, CPU, warm, 1 call | 138 ms | M4 Pro; p90 ~256 ms |
| Laya, MPS (GPU), batch 16 | ~400 ms/row | slower than CPU here; abandoned |
| Laya, cold process load | 2.5–5.4 s | weights from local cache |

Bouncer spawns a fresh process per tool call. Laya's warm speed is only
available behind a resident server (`laya[serve]` ships one); without it
every call pays the cold load and is **slower than Haiku**.

## The work-machine verdict

**It could run on a locked-down machine; it would not be worth running
there.** Measured on mini4; the work machine itself was not available.

- **On-disk size.** English weights `model.safetensors` 842,609,210 bytes
  (F32). `typed-decisions` is another 842 MB. Python runtime (venv with
  torch 2.14, transformers 5.17) 712 MB. **Total ~1.55 GB** for one
  checkpoint. Third-party GGUF quantizations exist at 431 MB (Q8_0) and
  401 MB (Q4_K_M) — not tested.
- **Runtime.** PyTorch + transformers, Python ≥3.10. **No Ollama.** The
  shipped ONNX path (`laya[onnx]`) still needs transformers for the
  tokenizer and a `.onnx` file you must export yourself with torch. A
  Node.js ONNX runner (`@receptron/laya`, MIT) avoids Python at run time
  but downloads 1.7 GB of fp32 ONNX. The GGUF runs on **ggmlc**, a custom
  compiler/runtime — *not* llama.cpp. Neither alternative was tested.
- **CPU-only.** Yes. 138 ms/call warm on an M4 Pro CPU; a work laptop CPU
  will be slower — not measured.
- **Admin rights.** Not needed in principle: `uv venv` + `pip install
  laya` into a home directory, weights into `~/.cache/huggingface`. Needs
  outbound access to PyPI and huggingface.co, which a locked-down network
  may block. **It is not a single binary plus a weights file** — the
  closest thing is the unverified ggmlc GGUF build.
- **Resident process.** To be useful as a per-call gate it needs a
  long-running local server. Whether the work machine permits a
  user-level daemon on a local port was not something I could check.
- **License.** The `laya` package ships the full Apache License 2.0 text
  (`laya-0.3.20.dist-info/licenses/LICENSE`). The weights repo declares
  `apache-2.0` in its metadata, is not gated, and has **no LICENSE file of
  its own**. Its base, ModernBERT-large, is declared `apache-2.0`. Apache-2.0
  as claimed; the weights rely on the metadata declaration.

## Recommendation

**Do not replace Haiku, and do not replace it behind a threshold.**
Zero-shot, both checkpoints are at chance on the one class that matters.
Trained on our own labels, Laya's encoder does worse than a bag of words,
so fine-tuning it further is not the cheap win the model card suggests:
the 421M model is not what would do the work. The speed and the
confidence score are real, but a confidence score over a classifier at
chance is not a gate.

What *would* change this: a permission-specific classifier trained on far
more DENYs than 432, with the project policy reachable in its context. That
is a different project, and the labels would need cleaning first — Haiku
disagrees with itself on a third of the repeated denials.

## What I could not verify

- The work machine: nothing was run there. Every work-machine statement is
  inferred from mini4 measurements and published documentation.
- The GGUF (ggmlc) and Node ONNX routes: documented, not run.
- Question wording: one phrasing only. A different wording could move the
  zero-shot numbers; with AUROC this close to 0.5 on two checkpoints, I
  would not expect it to move them to useful.
- Ground truth: none exists. "Recall" means agreement with Haiku.
- The probe: one regularisation strength (C=0.05), frozen encoder, no
  full fine-tune. A full fine-tune might do better; with 269 training
  DENYs and a frozen-encoder result below TF-IDF, I did not spend on it.
- Kev-9B, the named fallback: not tried. Laya was obtainable, and a 9B
  model fails the "small enough for work" half of the question on size
  alone.
